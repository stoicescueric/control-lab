# How to Use Control Lab

<Abstract>
How the lessons are laid out, what the two kinds of demo are for, and how progress tracking works. The
last section suggests a route through the site depending on whether you are building a robot this
season or reading for the theory.
</Abstract>

## How a lesson is laid out

Most lessons move in the same direction. They open with something going wrong on a robot: an arm that
sags, a drivetrain that arcs, a heading that drifts. Then there is a demo so you can see the behavior
before any equations show up. After that comes the math, built up from numbers you can check by hand,
then the Java that runs it, and finally the things the ideal model leaves out, like voltage sag,
backlash, latency, and wheel slip.

Section headings describe what the section is about, so you can skim the table of contents and jump to
the part you need.

The badge under each title (Easy, Medium, Hard) rates how much math the lesson uses. It says nothing
about how useful the lesson is. Some of the most important pages on the site are rated Easy.

## Two kinds of demo

Each demo panel has a label in its corner.

- **Live demo:** a running simulation. Drag targets, fire disturbances, change gains, and watch the
  system respond. If there are sliders, move them. The demos are there to be poked at.
- **Math explorer:** a graph of one equation. Nothing moves until you change a slider, and then the graph
  updates immediately. Use these to get a feel for a formula before the text works through it.

:::tip[Make it fullscreen]
Every demo panel has an expand button (⛶) in its corner. It helps on phones and when you are projecting
for the team. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to close it.
:::

Some live demos include a **Challenge**, a goal the simulation grades itself, such as "recover from the
wind gust within 3.5 s." Solved challenges turn gold and stay solved when you come back.

## Tracking your progress

The bottom of every lesson has a **Mark lesson as complete** button. Finished lessons get a green check
in the sidebar, and the homepage shows a *Continue where you left off* card. Progress lives in your
browser only. Nothing is uploaded, and clearing site data resets it.

## Suggested paths

**Building an FTC or FRC robot this season?** Read [Control & Feedback](./control-and-feedback.mdx),
bookmark [Conventions & Notation](../notation.mdx), and then work through this sequence:
[State Machines](../software-architecture/state-machines.mdx) →
[Feedforward](../control-theory/feedforward.mdx) → [PID](../control-theory/pid.mdx) →
[Mecanum Kinematics](../path-following/mecanum-kinematics.mdx) →
[Angle Wrapping](../path-following/angle-wrapping.mdx) →
[Pose Estimation Contract](../path-following/pose-estimation-contract.mdx) →
[Common Implementation](../path-following/common-implementation.mdx) →
[PID-to-Point](../path-following/pid-to-point.mdx). The other path followers and the sensor-fusion
lessons can wait until your robot needs them.

**Here for the theory?** Go through the modules in order. The controllers come first, then the
[Signal Processing](../signal-processing/index.mdx) track, which starts from a single noisy sensor and
ends at the Kalman filter, then path following. [Advanced Topics](../advanced-topics/index.mdx) has the
longer derivations that the main lessons point to.

**Lost in a symbol?** Every convention and recurring symbol is defined on
[Conventions & Notation](../notation.mdx).
