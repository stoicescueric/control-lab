# How to Use Control Lab

<Abstract>
Sixty seconds of orientation: how every lesson is structured, what the two kinds of interactive demo
are for, and how to track your progress — plus a suggested path through the curriculum depending on
whether you're an FTC/FRC programmer or here for the theory.
</Abstract>

## The anatomy of a lesson

Every substantive lesson follows the same arc, in order:

1. **A real failure.** Each lesson opens with a *Problem* — a mechanism misbehaving in a way you've
   probably seen: an arm that sags, an auto that overshoots, a heading controller that spins the long
   way around.
2. **See it before you derive it.** A demo or figure builds the intuition *before* the math, and the
   derivation explains every non-obvious variable next to the equation that uses it.
3. **Deploy it.** Lessons end with clean FTC Java that fits a real OpMode — measured loop times, real
   units, non-blocking loops — plus *hardware reality* warnings about where the ideal model bends.

The **difficulty badge** under each title (Easy / Medium / Hard) rates the math, not the value — some
of the most important lessons are Easy.

## Two kinds of interactive demo

Look at the pill in each panel's corner:

- **Live demo** — a real-time simulation. Physics runs continuously; drag targets, fire disturbances,
  tune gains, and watch the system respond. If it has sliders, move them — the demos are the lab bench.
- **Math explorer** — a Desmos-style graph of one equation. Nothing animates on its own; the graph
  responds instantly to the sliders so you can *feel* how the formula behaves before reading on.

:::tip[Make it fullscreen]
Every demo panel has an expand button (⛶) in its corner — useful on phones and when projecting for a
team. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to close.
:::

Some live demos carry a **🏁 Challenge** — a measurable goal graded by the simulation itself ("recover
from the wind gust within 3.5 s"). Passed challenges turn gold and stay solved between visits.

## Tracking your progress

At the bottom of every lesson is a **"Mark lesson as complete"** button. Completed lessons get a green
check in the sidebar, and the homepage shows a *Continue where you left off* card. Progress is stored
in your browser only — nothing is uploaded, and clearing site data resets it.

## Suggested paths

**Building an FTC/FRC robot this season?** Go in module order, but don't stall in the preface:
skim [Why Math Matters](./why-math-matters/index.mdx), then live in
[State Machines](../software-architecture/state-machines.mdx) →
[PID](../control-theory/pid.mdx) → [Feedforward](../control-theory/feedforward.mdx) →
[Odometry](../localization-odometry/odometry.mdx) →
[Mecanum Kinematics](../path-following/mecanum-kinematics.mdx) →
[PID-to-Point](../path-following/pid-to-point.mdx). Return for the rest when your robot demands it.

**Here for the theory?** Read the preface fully (the math lenses matter), then go straight through
the modules in order: controllers first, then the pose you control toward, then the
[Signal Processing](../signal-processing/index.mdx) track — which builds from a noisy sensor to the
extended Kalman filter, fusing the odometry you just learned — and finish with
[State-Space Control](../state-space-control/index.mdx), where the modeling threads reunite. The
[Advanced Topics](../advanced-topics/index.mdx) section holds the proofs and derivations the main
lessons reference.

**Lost in a symbol?** Every convention and recurring symbol lives on one page:
[Conventions & Notation](../notation.mdx).
